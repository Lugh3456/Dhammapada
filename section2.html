咄哉何为寐？螉螺蚌蠹类，隐弊以不净，迷惑计为身。焉有被斫创，心如婴疾痛，遘于众厄难，而反为用眠？思而不放逸，为仁学仁迹，从是无有忧，常念自灭意。正见学务增，是为世间明，所生福千倍，终不堕恶道。莫学小道，以信邪见，莫习放荡，令增欲意。善修法行，学诵莫犯，行道无忧，世世常安。慜学摄身，常慎思言，是到不死，行灭得安。非务勿学，是务宜行，已知可念，则漏得灭。见法利身，夫到善方，知利健行，是谓贤明。起觉义者，学灭以固，着灭自恣，损而不兴。是向以强，是学得中，从是解义，宜忆念行。学先断母，率君二臣，废诸营从，是上道人。学无朋类，不得善友，宁独守善，不与愚偕。乐戒学行，奚用伴为？独善无忧，如空野象。戒闻俱善，二者孰贤？方戒称闻，宜谛学行。学先护戒，开闭必固，施而无受，仂行勿卧。若人寿百岁，邪学志不善，不如生一日，精进受正法。若人寿百岁，奉火修异术，不如须臾顷，事戒者福称。能行说之可，不能勿空语，虚伪无诚信，智者所屏弃。学当先求解，观察别是非，受谛应诲彼，慧然不复惑。被发学邪道，草衣内贪浊，曚曚不识真，如聋听五音。学能舍三恶，以药消众毒，健夫度生死，如蛇脱故皮。学而多闻，持戒不失，两世见誉，所愿者得。学而寡闻，持戒不完，两世受痛，丧其本愿。夫学有二，常亲多闻，安谛解义，虽困不邪。稊稗害禾，多欲妨学，耘除众恶，成收必多。虑而后言，辞不强梁，法说义说，言而莫违。善学无犯，畏法晓忌，见微知者，诫无后患。远舍罪福，务成梵行，终身自摄，是名善学。

逐句解释

咄哉何为寐？螉螺蚌蠹类，隐弊以不净，迷惑计为身。

哎，为何还在沉睡？如同虫螺蚌蛀，以不净之物遮蔽自身，迷惑地把这当作是'我的身体'。

Alas, why do you sleep? Like grubs, snails, clams, and worms — concealed in impurity, deluded into thinking this is the self.

The chapter opens with a sharp rebuke. The sleeping person is compared to creatures that live entirely inside their shells or burrows — unaware, enclosed, mistaking their container for themselves. The rebuke is compassionate: wake up, because you can.

焉有被斫创，心如婴疾痛，遘于众厄难，而反为用眠？

身受刀创，心如患了重病一般痛苦，遭遇种种厄难，为何反而还在沉睡？

Struck by a blade, afflicted at heart like one in agony, beset by every danger — and yet you sleep?

The urgency is physical: sleeping through pain is not peace — it is numbness. The Buddha insists on wakefulness because reality, including suffering, requires a clear response. Anesthesia is not the answer.

思而不放逸，为仁学仁迹，从是无有忧，常念自灭意。

勤思正法，不放逸懈怠，修习慈仁，追随仁者的足迹，这样便无忧虑，常以正念灭除妄念。

Reflect and do not be heedless; learn kindness and follow the path of the kind — from this comes freedom from sorrow, ever stilling the mind through right recollection.

The alternative to heedlessness is not simply more effort but right effort — reflection, kindness, mindfulness. These three together cut the roots of anxiety. The mind that is well-tended does not require suppression; its turbulence quiets naturally.

正见学务增，是为世间明；所生福千倍，终不堕恶道。

以正见学习，精进不断增长，这是世间的明灯；所获得的福德千倍增加，终究不会堕入恶道。

Training with right view and ever-increasing diligence — this is a light for the world; merit grows a thousandfold, and one never falls to an evil realm.

Right view is foundational — not because belief matters more than practice, but because seeing accurately determines what we actually practise. Practice built on wrong view is like walking confidently in the wrong direction.

莫学小道，以信邪见，莫习放荡，令增欲意。

不要学习小道，不要相信邪见，不要习于放荡，以免增长贪欲之心。

Do not follow lesser paths; do not trust in wrong views; do not practise licentiousness, which only feeds desire.

The warning is against three forms of going astray: the wrong path, wrong views, and sensual indulgence. Each feeds the others — wrong views justify the wrong path, which enables indulgence, which deepens wrong views.

善修法行，学诵莫犯，行道无忧，世世常安。

善加修习法行，学习诵读而不违犯，行于正道则无忧无虑，世世代代常得安乐。

Train well in the conduct of the Dharma; study and recite without transgressing — walking the path, one is free from worry, and finds peace lifetime after lifetime.

The three practices named — conduct, study, non-transgression — map onto the traditional threefold training of sila, sutra, and precept. Their fruit is not just present peace but sustained wellbeing across the arc of existence.

慜学摄身，常慎思言，是到不死，行灭得安。

勤学摄护身行，常常谨慎思惟言语，这是到达不死之境的道路，修行灭尽而得安乐。

Diligently learn to restrain the body; always reflect carefully on speech — this leads to the deathless; through the practice of cessation one finds peace.

The 'deathless' (amata) is Nibbana — not the permanent survival of the self but the end of the process of compulsive arising and perishing. Restraint of body and care of speech are the visible, daily disciplines that prepare the ground for this deepest freedom.

非务勿学，是务宜行，已知可念，则漏得灭。

不是真正重要之事就不必学，真正重要之事应当去实行；已经明白的就应当忆念奉持，如此则烦恼漏尽。

Do not study what is not one's proper task; what is one's proper task, practise it — what is already understood, hold it in mind; in this way the taints are extinguished.

A teaching about economy of effort: study not every interesting thing, but the things that lead directly to liberation. Understanding without practice is accumulation without transformation. The taints (asava) are the deep-seated defilements — sensuality, becoming, ignorance — whose cessation marks awakening.

见法利身，夫到善方，知利健行，是谓贤明。

见到正法对身心有益，便到达善处；明白什么真正有利，并勇健地去行，这才是真正的贤明。

Seeing the Dharma as beneficial to oneself and reaching the good destination — knowing what is truly beneficial and vigorously practising it — this is true wisdom.

Wisdom here is not theoretical intelligence but the capacity to see what actually leads to wellbeing and then act on it. The connection between knowing and doing is essential: knowledge that does not translate into action is not yet wisdom.

起觉义者，学灭以固；着灭自恣，损而不兴。

能够唤起觉悟之义的人，以修学灭尽（烦恼）为坚固之道；若执著于灭而自我放纵，则只会损减而不能进步。

One who rouses the meaning of awakening trains firmly in cessation; clinging to cessation with self-indulgence leads to diminishment, not growth.

Even the goal of cessation can become an object of clinging — a subtle trap. The person who clings to the concept of Nibbana as a possession, while using it to justify non-effort ('I am already liberated'), declines rather than advances.

是向以强，是学得中，从是解义，宜忆念行。

以这样的方向坚定前行，通过这样的学习得到适中之道，由此理解法义，应当忆念奉行。

Press on in this direction with strength; through this learning find the middle way; from this understand the meaning of the teaching and hold it in mindful practice.

The middle way is invoked here not just as a philosophical position but as a practical orientation — neither too tight nor too loose, neither striving nor drifting. Mindful practice keeps the practitioner on this edge.

学先断母，率君二臣，废诸营从，是上道人。

修学者应先断除母贪（贪欲），再断除如君主般的慢（我慢）和两位臣子，废弃种种随从，这才是最上等的修道者。

The learner first cuts the mother — craving — then the lord and two ministers; abandoning the whole retinue, such a one is a person of the highest path.

This is a Pali-tradition image: craving (tanha) as the 'mother' that gives birth to continued existence; conceit as the 'lord'; and the defilements as the court retinue. The metaphor makes vivid how these mental factors maintain their hold through an elaborate social structure within the mind.

学无朋类，不得善友，宁独守善，不与愚偕。

在修学中若找不到志同道合的伴侣，得不到善友，宁愿独自守持善法，也不要与愚者为伍。

If in one's training one finds no companions and cannot find good friends, it is better to live alone in virtue than to keep company with fools.

This teaching recurs in several Buddhist texts and reflects hard experience: bad companionship can undo years of practice. The standard for 'good friend' (kalyana mitta) is high — someone whose own virtue, understanding, and care actively supports one's path.

乐戒学行，奚用伴为？独善无忧，如空野象。

若能乐于持戒、修学力行，又何需同伴呢？独自守善，无忧无虑，就像独行旷野的大象一样自在。

Delighting in precepts, study and practice — what need of companions? One who is good alone and without worry is like an elephant roaming the wilderness.

The elephant in the forest is a classic image of the meditator in solitude — large enough to be secure, self-sufficient, unbothered by the noise and interference of the herd. Solitary practice, when one is genuinely ready for it, has a particular quality of freedom.

戒闻俱善，二者孰贤？方戒称闻，宜谛学行。

持戒和多闻都是好的，这两者哪个更贤？与戒相应的多闻，才值得称赞；应当认真修学实行。

Both virtue and learning are good — which is the wiser of the two? Virtue that is matched by learning is to be praised; one should study and practise earnestly.

The question anticipates a common debate. The answer is that neither virtue alone nor learning alone is sufficient — each depends on the other. Virtue without understanding can be mechanical or misdirected; learning without virtue is merely intellectual accumulation.

学先护戒，开闭必固，施而无受，仂行勿卧。

修学首先要护持戒律，开关（感官之门）务必坚固；布施而不求回报，勤力修行不要懈怠贪睡。

In learning, first protect the precepts; firmly control what is opened and closed; give without receiving in return; strive in practice and do not lie about in sleep.

Four practical disciplines for the student: precepts, sense restraint, generosity without expectation, and industrious practice. Together they form the daily structure within which deeper understanding becomes possible.

若人寿百岁，邪学志不善，不如生一日，精进受正法。

若一个人活到百岁，学习邪法，志向不善，不如只活一天，却精进地受持正法。

If one lives a hundred years but studies wrongly with an unvirtuous intent, it is better to live a single day striving earnestly and receiving the true Dharma.

This verse pattern — 'a hundred years versus one day' — recurs throughout the Dhammapada. It is consistently making the same argument: quality of engagement matters infinitely more than duration. One day of genuine practice outweighs a century of sleep.

若人寿百岁，奉火修异术，不如须臾顷，事戒者福称。

若一个人活到百岁，供奉火祭、修习外道之法，不如片刻之间，奉持戒律者所积累的福德。

If one lives a hundred years worshipping fire and practising foreign rites, it does not match even a moment of the merit of one who keeps the precepts.

Fire sacrifice was a prominent Brahmanical practice in the Buddha's time. The verse does not attack the practitioners personally but makes a comparative claim: the merit of ethical discipline, which is internal and transforms the mind, exceeds that of external ritual.

能行说之可，不能勿空语，虚伪无诚信，智者所屏弃。

能做到的才可以说，做不到的就不要空口说白话；虚伪而无诚信，是有智慧的人所摒弃的。

If you can do it, speak of it; if you cannot, do not speak empty words — the wise reject those who are deceitful and lack sincerity.

Integrity between speech and action is fundamental to Buddhist ethics. The teacher who claims to teach what they have not practised, or the practitioner who describes attainments they have not reached, damages both themselves and those who trust them.

学当先求解，观察别是非，受谛应诲彼，慧然不复惑。

修学时应当先求理解，观察辨别是非，接受真理并能够以此教导他人，如此智慧光明，不再迷惑。

In learning, first seek understanding; observe and distinguish right from wrong; receive the truth and teach it to others accordingly — then wisdom shines and delusion ends.

The movement described is classical: receive the teaching, understand it, verify it through investigation, then embody it enough to transmit it. The final condition — that delusion ends — distinguishes genuine understanding from mere intellectual familiarity.

被发学邪道，草衣内贪浊，曚曚不识真，如聋听五音。

披散头发修学邪道，身穿草衣而内心充满贪欲和污浊，懵懵懂懂不识真理，就像聋人听五音一样毫无所得。

Matted hair, learning the wrong path; wearing grass robes while inwardly full of greed and defilement — muddled, unable to recognize truth, like a deaf person trying to hear five notes of music.

The image is of ascetics who adopt external signs of renunciation while their inner life remains untouched. Long hair, rough clothing, and austerities are visible — internal transformation is not. The deaf musician simile is precise: the apparatus of engagement is present, but the capacity to receive is absent.

学能舍三恶，以药消众毒，健夫度生死，如蛇脱故皮。

修学能够舍弃三恶（贪嗔痴），如同以药消解众毒；这样的修行者能度越生死，就像蛇蜕去旧皮一样。

Learning enables one to cast off the three evils, dissolving many poisons as medicine does — the vigorous man crosses over birth and death as a snake sheds its old skin.

The three evils — greed, hatred, delusion — are here compared to poisons, and learning/practice to medicine. The snake simile is among the most ancient in Indian thought: renewal through complete release of the old form. The practitioner who truly learns does not improve the old self — they shed it.

学而多闻，持戒不失，两世见誉，所愿者得。

广学多闻，持守戒律不失，此生和来世都获得称誉，所愿之事皆能实现。

One who learns widely and does not lose the precepts earns praise in both worlds, and gains what they aspire to.

The verse acknowledges that virtue and learning together produce what people actually want: reputation, respect, and the fulfilment of legitimate aspirations. The Buddhist path is not presented as requiring the sacrifice of all human goods, but as the most reliable way to actually attain them.

学而寡闻，持戒不完，两世受痛，丧其本愿。

学习但见闻浅薄，持戒又不完整，此生和来世都受苦痛，失去本有的善愿。

One who learns poorly and keeps the precepts imperfectly suffers in both worlds and loses what they originally aspired to.

The mirror of the previous verse. Incomplete effort produces incomplete results — not punishment, but the natural consequence of not building a complete foundation. The 'original aspiration' is worth noting: the practitioner had a genuine intention that was abandoned in practice.

夫学有二，常亲多闻，安谛解义，虽困不邪。

修学有两要：常亲近多闻善知识，安住于真谛、理解法义，即使处于困境也不偏邪。

Learning has two parts: always associating with those of wide learning, and calmly and truly understanding the meaning — even in hardship, one does not go astray.

The two aspects of learning — external (good companionship and exposure to teaching) and internal (genuine comprehension of meaning) — must work together. External learning without interior understanding is borrowed knowledge; inner aspiration without good teachers often goes in circles.

稊稗害禾，多欲妨学，耘除众恶，成收必多。

稗草伤害稻禾，多欲妨碍修学；除去种种恶的草，收成必然丰盛。

Weeds harm the grain; excessive desire hinders learning. Weed out all evils — the harvest will certainly be great.

The agricultural metaphor is exact: weeds do not argue with the grain, they simply crowd it out and compete for the same resources. Desire, distraction, and wrong thought do not oppose practice dramatically — they simply consume the time and energy that practice requires.

虑而后言，辞不强梁，法说义说，言而莫违。

说话之前先思考，言辞不强硬蛮横，说法说义，言出不违（言行一致）。

Reflect before speaking; let words not be harsh and overbearing; speak of the Dharma and its meaning; do not contradict what you say with your actions.

Careful speech is not merely politeness — it is a form of practice. Words spoken before thinking often land wrong. Harsh words damage relationships and close minds. And integrity between what one says and what one does is the foundation of any genuine teaching relationship.

善学无犯，畏法晓忌，见微知者，诫无后患。

善于修学的人不违犯戒律，敬畏正法、明白禁忌，能见微知著的人，及时告诫自己从而无后患。

One who learns well does not transgress; fearing the Dharma and knowing what is forbidden, seeing the subtle — such a person cautions themselves and has no future regret.

Restraint is not fear-based compliance but wisdom-based foresight. The person who can see the small beginnings of harm before they develop into larger problems is the one who avoids those problems altogether.

远舍罪福，务成梵行，终身自摄，是名善学。

远离罪与福的两端，致力于清净的梵行，终身自我约束，这才叫做善于修学。

Going beyond sin and merit alike, devoting oneself to the holy life, restraining oneself for an entire lifetime — this is called learning well.

The highest level: going beyond even the accumulation of merit as a goal. The mature practitioner is not keeping score — they are simply living the life of practice as fully and continuously as possible. This integration of practice into the whole of a life is the final mark of genuine learning.

总结 · Summary

教学品二十九颂，讲述真正修学之道：从警觉懈怠、亲近善友、护持戒律、广学多闻，到言行一致、持戒精进、最终超越善恶两边以成就梵行。真正的修学不是外在的苦行装扮，而是内心的清净与觉醒，持续一生的自我约束与正念。

The chapter on Learning offers twenty-nine verses on what genuine study and practice require. It begins by challenging sloth, describes the qualities of a good learner — right view, restraint, good companionship, integrity between speech and action — and culminates in the vision of practice that transcends even the accumulation of merit. True learning is not external accomplishment but the continuous and whole-life integration of wisdom, virtue, and mindfulness.
