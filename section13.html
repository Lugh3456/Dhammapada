第十三章 · 愚闇品

法句经 · Dhammapada

← 第十二章 目录 Contents 第十四章 →

不寐夜长，疲惓道长，愚生死长，莫知正法。痴意常冥，逝如流川，在一行强，独而无偶。愚人着数，忧戚久长，与愚居苦，于我犹怨。有子有财，愚惟汲汲，我且非我，何忧子财？暑当止此，寒当止此，愚多务虑，莫知来变。愚曚愚极，自谓我智，愚而胜智，是谓极愚。顽闇近智，如瓢斟味，虽久狎习，犹不知法。开达近智，如舌尝味，虽须臾习，即解道要。愚人施行，为身招患，快心作恶，自致重殃。行为不善，退见悔吝，致涕流面，报由宿习。行为德善，进睹欢喜，应来受福，喜笑悦习。过罪未熟，愚以恬惔，至其熟处，自受大罪。愚所望处，不谓适苦，临堕厄地，乃知不善。愚蠢作恶，不能自解，殃追自焚，罪成炽燃。愚好美食，月月滋甚，于十六分，未一思法。愚生念虑，至终无利，自招刀杖，报有印章。观处知其愚，不施而广求，所堕无道智，往往有恶行。远道近欲者，为食在学名，贪猗家居故，多取供异姓。学莫堕二望，莫作家沙门，贪家违圣教，为后自匮乏。此行与愚同，但令欲慢增，利求之愿异，求道意亦异。是以有识者，出为佛弟子，弃爱舍世习，终不堕生死。

逐句解释

不寐夜长，疲惓道长，愚生死长，莫知正法。

不能入眠时夜晚显得漫长，疲倦时道路显得漫长，愚者的生死轮回漫长，不知道正法。

For the sleepless, the night is long; for the weary, the road is long; for the fool who does not know the true Dharma, the cycle of birth and death is long.

Three forms of length: the sleepless night, the weary road, and the long cycle of rebirth for the ignorant. The verse is not punitive — it simply describes what happens when the mind lacks the Dharma as its orientation.

痴意常冥，逝如流川，在一行强，独而无偶。

愚痴的心意常处于黑暗，流逝如同奔流的川河，守住一种行持坚强，孤独而无伴侣。

The foolish mind is always in darkness, flowing away like a river; holding to one practice with strength — alone and without companion.

Darkness and flowing away: the unawakened mind is both obscured and in constant motion. The instruction — hold to one practice with strength — points toward the stability of concentrated practice.

愚人着数，忧戚久长，与愚居苦，于我犹怨。

愚人执著于计数，忧愁苦恼长久，与愚人同住是苦，对我犹如怨仇。

The fool clings to enumerations; sorrow and distress are long; dwelling with fools is suffering; it is to me like living with an enemy.

Clinging to tallies and scores (著数) is one form of foolishness — keeping track of status, possessions, achievements. Such preoccupation produces the sorrow that makes foolish company as burdensome as an enemy.

有子有财，愚惟汲汲，我且非我，何忧子财？

有子女有财富，愚人为此汲汲营营，然而自我且非真我，何必为子女财富而忧愁？

'I have children, I have wealth' — the fool labours anxiously over these; but the self is not truly a self — why worry over children and wealth?

The anatta (non-self) insight applied to worry: the 'owner' who is anxious about possessions is itself not what it takes itself to be. The anxiety is built on a misunderstanding.

暑当止此，寒当止此，愚多务虑，莫知来变。

'夏天就在这里，冬天也在这里'，愚者有很多事务要考虑，却不知道未来的变化。

'In summer I will stay here; in winter I will stay here' — the fool has many concerns and plans, not knowing the changes that are coming.

Planning without wisdom: the fool projects stability into an inherently unstable future, arranging summer and winter accommodations while not knowing they may not live to occupy them.

愚曚愚极，自谓我智，愚而胜智，是谓极愚。

愚痴之极、愚蒙之极，却自称有智慧，愚者以为胜过智者，这才是最极端的愚痴。

Utterly foolish and deluded, yet claiming to be wise — a fool who thinks themselves superior to the wise is called supremely foolish.

The compound foolishness: ignorance is one thing; ignorance about one's ignorance — the Dunning-Kruger problem — is far worse, because it closes the very door to correction.

顽闇近智，如瓢斟味，虽久狎习，犹不知法。

顽钝愚暗的人靠近有智慧的人，就像用木瓢来尝味道，虽然长久亲近习处，仍然不知正法。

The dull and darkened person near the wise is like a ladle tasting a sauce; though long familiar, they still do not know the Dharma.

The ladle in the sauce: it touches the food but cannot taste. Proximity to wisdom does not automatically produce understanding — the capacity to receive must itself be cultivated.

开达近智，如舌尝味，虽须臾习，即解道要。

开通豁达的人靠近有智慧的人，就像舌头尝味道，即使只是片刻的亲近习处，便能理解道的要义。

The open and perceptive person near the wise is like a tongue tasting a sauce; even in a moment's association, they immediately grasp the essentials of the path.

The tongue versus the ladle: the difference is not duration but quality of reception. Genuine openness can receive the teaching immediately; the closed mind may sit for years and learn nothing.

愚人施行，为身招患，快心作恶，自致重殃。

愚人的所作所为，为自身招来祸患，以快心作恶，自招严重的灾殃。

The fool's conduct invites harm upon themselves; delighting in the heart, they do evil and bring heavy calamity upon themselves.

The mechanics of self-harm through foolish action: delight in evil feels good in the moment but sets in motion the chain of consequences that results in heavy calamity.

行为不善，退见悔吝，致涕流面，报由宿习。

行为不善，事后见到悔恨，以致涕泪纵横，果报由宿世的习气而来。

Acting unwholesomely, one afterward sees regret and remorse, with tears flowing — the consequence comes from past habituation.

Regret after the fact is painful but also important: it shows the moral faculty is still functioning. The tears are evidence that the person knows, at some level, what they have done.

行为德善，进睹欢喜，应来受福，喜笑悦习。

行为有德行善，向前见到欢喜，应当来受的福报，以喜笑悦乐为习。

Acting with virtue and goodness, one looks ahead and sees joy; coming to receive the merit one is due — delight and laughter become one's habit.

The forward-looking aspect of virtue: the virtuous person looks to the future with anticipation rather than dread. The habit of joy follows naturally from the habit of good action.

过罪未熟，愚以恬惔，至其熟处，自受大罪。

过失和罪业尚未成熟，愚者因此安然，待到成熟之时，自己承受大的罪报。

While past faults and sins have not yet ripened, the fool is at ease; when they do ripen, one bears the great sin oneself.

The delayed consequence: karma is compared to ripening fruit. The fool mistakes the unripened period (no visible consequence) for immunity. The ripening comes eventually.

愚所望处，不谓适苦，临堕厄地，乃知不善。

愚者所期望之处，不认为会走向苦，直到临近堕入危险之地，才知道是不善的。

Where the fool hopes to reach, they do not think will be difficult; only when about to fall into a dangerous place do they realize it is unwholesome.

The moment of recognition comes too late: the fool only sees the problem when falling into it. This is why the teaching always emphasizes foresight — seeing danger before it arrives.

愚蠢作恶，不能自解，殃追自焚，罪成炽燃。

愚蠢者作恶，不能自行解脱，灾殃追来而自焚，罪业成就而炽然燃烧。

The foolish do evil and cannot liberate themselves; calamity pursues them and burns them; sin is accomplished and blazes fiercely.

The burning image for accumulated sin: it does not merely weigh down but actively burns — the metaphor captures the ongoing suffering of bad conscience and bad karma combined.

愚好美食，月月滋甚，于十六分，未一思法。

愚者喜爱美食，月月滋甚愈加，在十六分之中，没有一分用来思惟正法。

The fool loves fine food, growing more so month by month; of sixteen portions of attention, not one is given to reflecting on the Dharma.

The fraction of attention given to the Dharma: the verse uses the image of sixteen portions to make the fool's priorities precise. Not one sixteenth of attention goes to what matters most.

愚生念虑，至终无利，自招刀杖，报有印章。

愚者一生念虑，到头来毫无利益，自招刀杖之患，果报如同盖有印章一样确定。

The fool lives in worry and care; to the end there is no benefit; they invite upon themselves rods and blows; the consequence is as certain as a sealed stamp.

The sealed stamp: karma's consequence is not tentative or conditional. The fool's worry produces no benefit and generates harm as reliably as a legal seal confirms a document.

观处知其愚，不施而广求，所堕无道智，往往有恶行。

观察一个人的所在处便知其愚痴，不布施而广泛求取，所堕落的是无道无智的境地，处处有恶行。

By observing where they are, one knows their foolishness; not giving while seeking widely; they fall to a place of no path and no wisdom; everywhere their conduct is evil.

The environmental diagnostic: where someone is — their relationships, their community, their mental states — reveals their wisdom or foolishness. The fool who takes without giving inhabits a shrinking world.

远道近欲者，为食在学名，贪猗家居故，多取供异姓。

远离道而近于欲者，为了饮食而在学名，贪著依猗家居的缘故，多取财物供给外族。

Those far from the path and near to desire; in their learning they are after reputation for food; clinging to the householder's life, they take much to give to outsiders.

The corruption of learning through wrong motivation: studying for reputation and material support, rather than for liberation. The symptoms are visible in behavior — taking much, giving wrongly.

学莫堕二望，莫作家沙门，贪家违圣教，为后自匮乏。

修学不要堕入两种期望，不要成为在家的沙门，贪著家居而违背圣教，将来自招匮乏。

In learning, do not fall into two expectations; do not become a householder-monk; craving household life and violating the noble teaching leads to future destitution.

The hybrid life — neither fully renounced nor honestly lay — is criticized here as the worst of both worlds, producing neither the householder's legitimate enjoyment nor the renunciant's liberation.

此行与愚同，但令欲慢增，利求之愿异，求道意亦异。

这种行为与愚痴相同，只是让贪欲和我慢增长，求利的愿望不同，求道的意志也不同。

Such conduct is the same as that of fools — it only increases craving and pride; the wish for profit is different from the will to seek the path.

The chapter closes by distinguishing two fundamentally different orientations: the wish for profit (which increases craving) and the will to seek the path (which diminishes it). These cannot be served simultaneously.

是以有识者，出为佛弟子，弃爱舍世习，终不堕生死。

因此有见识的人，出家成为佛弟子，舍弃爱欲、放弃世间习气，终究不再堕入生死。

Therefore the person of understanding goes forth as a disciple of the Buddha; abandoning love and leaving the habits of the world, they will finally not fall back into birth and death.

The invitation and its consequence: understanding the fool's condition is not the end of the chapter's teaching — it is the beginning of the path. The person who genuinely sees goes forth, abandons, and is freed.

总结 · Summary

愚闇品二十一颂，全面描述愚者的特征与处境：不知正法、长处黑暗、执著计数、否认无我、盲目规划，以及最深的愚痴——自以为智。最精妙的是瓢与舌的对比：同样靠近智者，顽闇者如瓢不知味，开达者如舌即解要义。品末以有识者出家作结，揭示见到愚痴的自然结果是走向解脱。

The chapter on Fools presents twenty-one verses describing the fool's characteristic condition: ignorance of the Dharma, darkness of mind, obsessive planning without wisdom, and — most damaging — not knowing that one is foolish. The ladle-and-tongue contrast is memorable: both near wisdom, one receives nothing, the other receives everything in an instant. The chapter closes with the wise person going forth — seeing the fool's condition clearly enough to choose a different path.
