第十二章 · 华香品

法句经 · Dhammapada

← 第十一章 目录 Contents 第十三章 →

孰能择地，舍鉴取天，谁说法句，如择善华。学者择地，舍鉴取天，善说法句，能采德华。知世坏喻，幻法忽有，断魔华敷，不睹生死。见身如沫，幻法自然，断魔华敷，不睹生死。身病则萎，若华零落，死命来至，如水湍骤。贪欲无厌，消散人念，邪致之财，为自侵欺。如蜂集华，不娆色香，但取昧去，仁入聚然。不务观彼，作与不作，常自省身，知正不正。如可意华，色好无香，工语如是，不行无得。如可意华，色美且香，工语有行，必得其福。多作宝花，结步摇绮，广积德者，所生转好。奇草芳花，不逆风熏，近道敷开，德人逼香。旃檀多香，青莲芳花，虽曰是真，不如戒香。华香气微，不可谓真，持戒之香，到天殊胜。戒具成就，行无放逸，定意度脱，长离魔道。如作田沟，近于大道，中生莲华，香洁可意。有生死然，凡夫处边，慧者乐出，为佛弟子。

逐句解释

孰能择地，舍鉴取天，谁说法句，如择善华。

谁能选择地，放弃镜鉴而取天，谁能说法句，如同择取善华？

Who can choose the ground, leave the mirror and take to heaven? Who can speak the Dharma verses, as one selects fine flowers?

The opening questions establish the chapter's metaphor: the dharma-practitioner as someone who selects the finest flowers — not every bloom, but the best ones.

学者择地，舍鉴取天，善说法句，能采德华。

修学者能够择地，舍鉴而取天，善于说法句，能够采摘功德之花。

The learner chooses the ground, leaves the mirror for heaven; speaking the Dharma well, one can gather the flowers of virtue.

The learner is identified as the one who can do this: select wisely, transcend limitation, gather virtue. Practice is described as a kind of cultivation and harvest.

知世坏喻，幻法忽有，断魔华敷，不睹生死。

知道世间如器物坏灭的比喻，幻法如忽然有生，断除魔的花枝蔓延，不再见到生死。

Knowing the world through the simile of breaking; the illusory dharma arises suddenly; cutting Mara's flowering expansion, one no longer beholds birth and death.

The world as a breaking pot: impermanent, fragile, already in the process of dissolution. Seeing this clearly cuts the 'flowering' of Mara — the way craving proliferates endlessly.

见身如沫，幻法自然，断魔华敷，不睹生死。

见到身体如同水沫，幻法自然如此，断除魔的花枝蔓延，不再见到生死。

Seeing the body as foam; the illusory dharma is naturally so; cutting Mara's flowering expansion, one no longer beholds birth and death.

The body as sea-foam: beautiful to look at, ephemeral, without substance. This seeing is not pessimistic — it is liberating. When the body is seen accurately, Mara's hold on us through it dissolves.

身病则萎，若华零落，死命来至，如水湍骤。

身体生病则萎谢，就像花朵零落，死亡来临，如同急流飞湍。

When the body is sick it withers like a falling flower; death comes like rushing, rapid water.

Two speeds of death: the gradual (the flower wilting through illness) and the sudden (the rushing torrent). Both are inevitable; neither can be bargained with.

贪欲无厌，消散人念，邪致之财，为自侵欺。

贪欲无有厌足，消散人的正念，以邪道得来的财物，是在自我侵害和欺骗。

Craving knows no satisfaction; it disperses a person's right recollection; wealth gained through wrong means is self-harm and self-deception.

Craving's two consequences: it consumes mindfulness (the resource we need most) and when satisfied through wrong means, it corrupts the person who satisfies it.

如蜂集华，不娆色香，但取昧去，仁入聚然。

就像蜜蜂采集花朵，不扰乱花的色香，只取其甘味而去，仁者进入聚落也是如此。

Like a bee gathering flowers, not disturbing their colour or fragrance, only taking the sweetness and leaving — so the kind person enters a community.

The bee metaphor for the ethical practitioner: taking only what is freely given, leaving things undamaged. This is the ideal of non-exploitation as the basis for all community.

不务观彼，作与不作，常自省身，知正不正。

不注重观察他人，他人做与不做，常常自我反省，知道什么是正什么不正。

Not focused on observing others — what they do or don't do; always examining oneself, knowing what is right and what is not.

Self-examination as the proper direction of moral attention. The gaze that constantly evaluates others becomes an obstacle to the self-knowledge that is actually needed.

如可意华，色好无香，工语如是，不行无得。

就像令人喜爱的花朵，颜色美丽却无香气，言语华美但不付诸行动也无所得。

Like a beautiful flower, fair in colour but without fragrance — fine speech without action gains nothing.

Beauty without substance: the flower without fragrance is lovely but gives nothing essential. Speech without practice is the same — pleasant to hear, spiritually empty.

如可意华，色美且香，工语有行，必得其福。

就像令人喜爱的花朵，颜色美丽且有芬香，言语华美而付诸行动，必定得到福报。

Like a beautiful flower, fair in colour and fragrant too — fine speech with action must certainly gain merit.

The complete flower: beauty plus fragrance, speech plus practice. When action matches words, the full fruit appears.

多作宝花，结步摇绮，广积德者，所生转好。

多多采集宝贵的花，编成步摇华绮，广积功德者，所生处越来越好。

Gathering many precious flowers, weaving them into a garland — one who accumulates virtue widely is reborn in ever better conditions.

The garland metaphor for accumulated merit: each act of virtue is a flower, and together they form something beautiful that accompanies the practitioner into future lives.

奇草芳花，不逆风熏，近道敷开，德人逼香。

奇异的草和芳香的花，不能逆风而熏，靠近正道则开放，有德之人的香气能够逆风传播。

Rare grasses and fragrant flowers cannot send their scent against the wind; but one who keeps close to the path opens up — the fragrance of a virtuous person spreads against the wind.

The most celebrated comparison in this chapter: ordinary fragrance cannot travel against the wind, but the fragrance of genuine virtue travels in all directions, unimpeded.

旃檀多香，青莲芳花，虽曰是真，不如戒香。

旃檀香木多有芳香，青莲花芬芳美丽，虽然说这些是真实的香，但不如戒律之香。

Sandalwood is fragrant; the blue lotus is sweet and fair — though called true fragrances, none match the fragrance of virtue.

Sandalwood and lotus were the finest fragrances known in ancient India. Yet the fragrance of virtue surpasses both — because it travels beyond physical limits and endures beyond the flower's life.

华香气微，不可谓真，持戒之香，到天殊胜。

花香气味微弱，不可称为真香，持戒之香，到达天界都是最为殊胜的。

The fragrance of flowers is faint and cannot be called true fragrance; the fragrance of keeping the precepts reaches even to heaven — supremely excellent.

Faint flower fragrance versus the fragrance of virtue that reaches heaven: the scale of virtue's fragrance is categorically different from any physical scent.

戒具成就，行无放逸，定意度脱，长离魔道。

戒律具足成就，行为不放逸，禅定意识度脱，长久远离魔道。

With virtue complete and accomplished, conduct without heedlessness, concentration and liberation — one is long removed from Mara's path.

The threefold training (virtue, concentration, wisdom/liberation) described as the complete path that removes one permanently from Mara's domain.

如作田沟，近于大道，中生莲华，香洁可意。

就像挖凿田沟，近于大道，其中生出莲花，香洁令人欢喜。

Like a ditch dug near a great road, in which a lotus grows — fragrant, pure, and pleasing.

The lotus in the ditch: beauty emerging from humble, even dirty conditions. Practice is possible anywhere — the lotus does not require a pristine pond to bloom.

有生死然，凡夫处边，慧者乐出，为佛弟子。

有生死如此，凡夫处于边际，有智慧的人乐于超出，成为佛弟子。

With birth and death being so, ordinary people dwell at the margins; the wise delight in going beyond — they are disciples of the Buddha.

The margins versus the centre: ordinary consciousness dwells at the periphery of what is possible. The Buddha's disciples move toward the centre — the direct experience of liberation.

总结 · Summary

华香品十七颂，以花与香为核心意象，对比世间之美与德行之美。花朵色美而香微，旃檀青莲虽为真香，仍不如持戒之香——因为戒香不受风向限制，直达天界。修行者如蜜蜂采花而不扰色香，如莲花出于田沟而清洁，积累功德如编制宝花花环，所生之处越来越美好。

The chapter on Flowers and Fragrance uses seventeen verses to explore the relationship between beauty, virtue, and liberation. Flowers provide the central metaphor: fine speech without action is a beautiful but scentless flower; virtue's fragrance surpasses all physical fragrances and travels against the wind. The practitioner is like a bee — gathering only sweetness without disturbing — and like a lotus growing in a ditch, beautiful regardless of conditions.
