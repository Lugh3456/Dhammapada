第十六章 · 述千品

法句经 · Dhammapada

← 第十五章 目录 Contents 第十七章 →

虽诵千言，句义不正，不如一要，闻可灭意。虽诵千言，不义何益，不如一义，闻行可度。虽多诵经，不解何益，解一法句，行可得道。千千为敌，一夫胜之，未若自胜，为战中上。自胜最贤，故曰人雄，护意调身，自损至终。虽曰尊天，神魔梵释，皆莫能胜，自胜之人。月千反祠，终身不辍，不如须臾，一心念法。一念道福，胜彼终身，虽终百岁，奉事火祠，不如须臾，供养三尊。一供养福，胜彼百年，祭神以求福，从后观其报，四分未望一，不如礼贤者。能善行礼节，常敬长老者，四福自然增，色力寿而安。若人寿百岁，远正不持戒，不如生一日，守戒正意禅。若人寿百岁，邪伪无有智，不如生一日，一心学正智。若人寿百岁，懈怠不精进，不如生一日，勉力行精进。若人寿百岁，不知成败事，不如生一日，见微知所忌。若人寿百岁，不见甘露道，不如生一日，服行甘露味。若人寿百岁，不知大道义，不如生一日，学推佛法要。

逐句解释

虽诵千言，句义不正，不如一要，闻可灭意。

虽然诵读千言万语，但句义不正确，不如听闻一句要义，能够灭除心意（的妄念）。

Though one recites a thousand words with meanings that are not right, one good essential verse heard — capable of stilling the mind — is better.

Quality over quantity: the thousand imperfect verses accomplish nothing; the single right verse, genuinely heard, changes the mind. The teaching is about reception, not recitation.

虽诵千言，不义何益，不如一义，闻行可度。

虽然诵读千言，无义何益？不如一句有义之语，听闻而行可以度越。

Though one recites a thousand words — what use if there is no meaning? One meaningful verse, heard and practised, can carry one across.

Meaning plus practice: hearing alone is not enough; the verse that is heard and acted upon is the only one that carries across. Recitation without application is sound without substance.

虽多诵经，不解何益，解一法句，行可得道。

虽然诵读很多经典，不理解有什么益处？理解一句法句，付诸行动可以得道。

Though one recites many sutras — what use without understanding? Understanding a single verse of the Dharma and practising it — one can attain the path.

The three-part sequence: understanding (not just hearing), a single verse (not thousands), and practice (not just understanding). Liberation requires all three working together.

千千为敌，一夫胜之，未若自胜，为战中上。

以千千之众为敌，一人胜过他们，不如自我战胜，这才是战争中最殊胜的。

A thousand thousand enemies — one man defeats them; but conquering oneself is superior to this — the highest victory in battle.

The most famous comparative in this chapter: military victory over armies is remarkable, but self-conquest is categorically greater because the self is both the most persistent enemy and the one whose defeat truly matters.

自胜最贤，故曰人雄，护意调身，自损至终。

自我战胜是最贤明的，因此称为人中英雄，守护心意调伏身体，自我损减直到终结（烦恼）。

Self-conquest is the wisest — therefore called a hero among people; guarding the mind and taming the body, diminishing the self until the end.

The hero defined not by external achievement but by the internal conquest of ego and craving. Diminishing the self — reducing its grip, its pride, its demands — is the work of the real hero.

虽曰尊天，神魔梵释，皆莫能胜，自胜之人。

即使尊贵的天神、魔王、梵天、帝释，都无法战胜自我战胜的人。

Though they be honoured gods, spirit, Mara, Brahma, or Indra — none can defeat a person who has conquered themselves.

The defeated ego cannot be re-defeated: even the most powerful divine and demonic forces in the cosmos cannot undo what genuine self-conquest has accomplished.

月千反祠，终身不辍，不如须臾，一心念法。

月月千次祭祀，终身不辍，不如片刻间，一心念法。

A thousand sacrifices a month, unceasing for an entire lifetime — not equal to a single moment of one-pointed mindfulness of the Dharma.

The sacrifice comparison: elaborate religious ritual throughout a lifetime cannot match a genuine moment of Dharma-mindfulness. This is not anti-religious but anti-empty-ritual.

一念道福，胜彼终身，虽终百岁，奉事火祠，不如须臾，供养三尊。

一念道的福德，胜过那终身祭祀，虽然终其一生奉事火祭，不如片刻供养三宝。

One moment of merit from the path surpasses a lifetime of that; though one worships fire for a hundred years of life, it is not equal to a moment of offering to the Three Jewels.

The Three Jewels (Buddha, Dharma, Sangha) as the supreme object of offering: not because ritual to them is more effective, but because they represent the path that leads to genuine transformation.

一供养福，胜彼百年，祭神以求福，从后观其报，四分未望一，不如礼贤者。

一次供养的福德，胜过那百年，以祭神求福，事后观其果报，四分之中未有一分，不如礼敬贤者。

One gift's merit surpasses that of a hundred years; worshipping spirits for merit — looking at the result afterward, not one quarter of what was hoped for; not equal to honouring the wise.

Pragmatic comparison of different forms of worship: honouring the genuinely wise produces better results than spirit-worship, measurably and practically.

能善行礼节，常敬长老者，四福自然增，色力寿而安。

能够善加行礼节，常常敬重长老，四种福报自然增长：色、力、寿、安。

One who properly practises courtesy and always honours elders — four blessings naturally increase: form, strength, lifespan, and peace.

The social virtue of reverence for elders, when genuine rather than performed, produces four blessings: physical vitality, strength, long life, and inner peace.

若人寿百岁，远正不持戒，不如生一日，守戒正意禅。

若一个人活到百岁，远离正法不持戒，不如只活一天，守戒正意而禅修。

If one lives a hundred years far from the right and not keeping precepts — better to live one day keeping precepts, with a correct mind in meditation.

One day of genuine practice versus a century of neglect: the verse is not about duration of life but quality of engagement. The single day of precepts and meditation contains more genuine life.

若人寿百岁，邪伪无有智，不如生一日，一心学正智。

若一个人活到百岁，邪伪而无智慧，不如只活一天，一心修学正智。

If one lives a hundred years in falsehood and without wisdom — better to live one day with one-pointed cultivation of right wisdom.

The pattern continues: a long life spent in falsehood and ignorance is less than one day of genuine wisdom-cultivation. Life is measured in understanding, not years.

若人寿百岁，懈怠不精进，不如生一日，勉力行精进。

若一个人活到百岁，懈怠而不精进，不如只活一天，勉力修行精进。

If one lives a hundred years in laziness without diligence — better to live one day striving vigorously.

Vigour against the hundred years of sloth: what makes a life is not its length but its energy and direction. One day of genuine effort counts for more than a century of drift.

若人寿百岁，不知成败事，不如生一日，见微知所忌。

若一个人活到百岁，不知道成败之事，不如只活一天，能见微知著而知所禁忌。

If one lives a hundred years not knowing what succeeds and what fails — better to live one day seeing the subtle and knowing what to avoid.

Discernment: seeing the subtle signs before they become obvious problems. A single day of this kind of seeing is more valuable than a century of oblivious living.

若人寿百岁，不见甘露道，不如生一日，服行甘露味。

若一个人活到百岁，不见甘露之道，不如只活一天，服行甘露的滋味。

If one lives a hundred years without seeing the path of nectar — better to live one day tasting and practising the nectar.

The nectar of the Dharma, once genuinely tasted, makes all other satisfactions seem thin. A single day of this tasting contains more of what life is for than a lifetime without it.

若人寿百岁，不知大道义，不如生一日，学推佛法要。

若一个人活到百岁，不知道大道的要义，不如只活一天，学习推究佛法的要义。

If one lives a hundred years not knowing the meaning of the great path — better to live one day studying and discerning the essentials of the Buddha's teaching.

The final and most encompassing: knowing the meaning of the great path. All the previous verses are specifications of this one. A day of this knowing contains the whole.

总结 · Summary

述千品十六颂，以'一胜于多'为贯穿全品的核心论点。一句要义胜于千言，一人自胜胜于征服千军，一念道福胜于终身祭祀，守戒一日胜于放逸百岁。真正重要的不是数量和时间，而是质量和理解。人生的价值在于理解和修行的深度，而非岁月的长度。

The chapter on Thousands presents sixteen verses on the principle that quality surpasses quantity in all spiritual matters. One right verse exceeds a thousand wrong ones; self-conquest exceeds defeating a thousand armies; one moment of Dharma-mindfulness exceeds a lifetime of ritual sacrifice; one day of genuine precepts and meditation exceeds a century of heedless life. The Dhammapada's message is not about doing more — it is about understanding and practising what actually matters.
