心为法本，心尊心使，中心念恶，即言即行，罪苦自追，车轹于辙。心为法本，心尊心使，中心念善，即言即行，福乐自追，如影随形。随乱意行，拘愚入冥，自大无法，何解善言。随正意行，开解清明，不为妒嫉，敏达善言。愠于怨者，未尝无怨，不愠自除，是道可宗。不好责彼，务自省身，如有知此，永灭无患。行见身净，不摄诸根，饮食不节，慢堕怯弱，为邪所制，如风靡草。观身不净，能摄诸根，食知节度，常乐精进，不为邪动，如风大山。不吐毒态，欲心驰骋，未能自调，不应法衣；能吐毒态，戒意安静，降心已调，此应法衣。以真为伪，以伪为真，是为邪计，不得真利；知真为真，见伪知伪，是为正计，必得真利。盖屋不密，天雨则漏，意不惟行，淫泆为穿；盖屋善密，雨则不漏，摄意惟行，淫泆不生。鄙夫染人，如近臭物，渐迷习非，不觉成恶；贤夫染人，如近香熏，进智习善，行成洁芳。造忧后忧，行恶两忧，彼忧惟惧，见罪心懅；造喜后喜，行善两喜，彼喜惟欢，见福心安。今悔后悔，为恶两悔，厥为自殃，受罪热恼；今欢后欢，为善两欢，厥为自祐，受福悦豫。巧言多求，放荡无戒，怀淫怒痴，不惟止观，聚如群牛，非佛弟子；时言少求，行道如法，除淫怒痴，觉正意解，见对不起，是佛弟子。

逐句解释

心为法本，心尊心使，中心念恶，即言即行，罪苦自追，车轹于辙。

心是一切法的根本，心是主宰，心在驱使，内心念恶，随即言恶行恶，罪与苦自然追随，就像车轮碾过轨辙一样。

Mind is the root of all dharmas; mind is sovereign, mind drives all; when the mind thinks evil, speech and action follow immediately — sin and suffering pursue one like a wheel following the track.

The opening of this chapter restates the most famous verse of the Dhammapada in the T0210's classical idiom. The wheel-and-track image: no escape once the groove is cut.

心为法本，心尊心使，中心念善，即言即行，福乐自追，如影随形。

心是一切法的根本，心是主宰，心在驱使，内心念善，随即言善行善，福与乐自然追随，就像影子跟随形体一样。

Mind is the root of all dharmas; mind is sovereign, mind drives all; when the mind thinks good, speech and action follow — blessing and joy pursue one like a shadow following the form.

The paired verse: the same mechanism, the same inevitability, but toward joy rather than suffering. The shadow never leaves the form; happiness never leaves the life of genuine virtue.

随乱意行，拘愚入冥，自大无法，何解善言。

随着散乱的心意而行，被愚痴拘禁而陷入黑暗，自大无法，怎能理解善言？

Following a disordered mind into action; imprisoned by folly in darkness; arrogant and lawless — how can such a person understand good speech?

The inverse of virtue: a disordered mind, folly, arrogance, and lawlessness form a closed system that cannot receive correction. The answer to 'how?' is: they cannot.

随正意行，开解清明，不为妒嫉，敏达善言。

随着正确的心意而行，开解清明，不被嫉妒所控，敏锐通达善言。

Following a correct mind into action; open, clear and bright; free from envy; quick to understand and reach good speech.

The open system: a correct mind, clarity, freedom from envy, and receptivity to wisdom mutually reinforce each other.

愠于怨者，未尝无怨，不愠自除，是道可宗。

对怨恨之人心生嗔怒，从未有消除怨恨的时候，不嗔怒则自然消除，这是可以信赖的道。

Those who are angered by enemies have never been without enmity; not being angered dissolves it by itself — this is a path worthy of trust.

The self-perpetuating nature of enmity: anger responds to anger and produces more anger. The interruption of this cycle — through non-reactivity — is a reliable path.

不好责彼，务自省身，如有知此，永灭无患。

不喜欢责怪别人，专注于自我反省，如果能明白这个道理，便能永远消灭无患。

Not liking to blame others; devoted to examining oneself — one who knows this will forever extinguish trouble and be free from harm.

Self-examination as the remedy for blame: pointing the finger outward is the habit; pointing it inward is the practice. The practitioner who masters this is free from the cycle of grievance.

行见身净，不摄诸根，饮食不节，慢堕怯弱，为邪所制，如风靡草。

行为上见到身体清净，却不摄护诸根，饮食不知节制，懈怠而怯弱，被邪恶所控制，就像风吹草倒一样。

Appearing to purify the body in conduct but not restraining the senses; immoderate in eating; lazy and cowardly; controlled by wrong forces — like grass flattened by wind.

The failure mode: external appearance of purity without internal restraint. The wind and grass image: whatever is not rooted is easily overcome.

观身不净，能摄诸根，食知节度，常乐精进，不为邪动，如风大山。

观察身体的不净，能够摄护诸根，饮食知道节制，常以精进为乐，不被邪恶所动，就像风吹不动大山。

Contemplating the body as impure; able to restrain the senses; moderate in eating; always delighting in diligence; not moved by wrong forces — like a great mountain in the wind.

The mountain in the wind: the same force that flattens unrooted grass cannot move what is deeply anchored. The practices named build exactly this kind of inner rootedness.

不吐毒态，欲心驰骋，未能自调，不应法衣，能吐毒态，戒意安静，降心已调，此应法衣。

不能吐出毒性心态，欲心驰骋，不能自我调伏，不相应于法衣；能够吐出毒性心态，戒意安静，降伏内心已经调伏，这才相应于法衣。

Unable to expel the toxic mind-state, with desires running wild and unable to tame oneself — such a one does not accord with the robe; able to expel the toxic, with virtuous intent calmed, the mind subdued — this accords with the robe.

The robe (法衣) as a symbol of genuine renunciation: it is verified by inner transformation, not just worn. The toxic mind-state is one saturated with greed, hatred, or delusion.

以真为伪，以伪为真，是为邪计，不得真利。知真为真，见伪知伪，是为正计，必得真利。

把真实当作虚假，把虚假当作真实，这是邪见，无法得到真实的利益；知道真实就是真实，见到虚假就知道是虚假，这是正见，必定得到真实的利益。

Taking the true as false and the false as true — this is wrong understanding, gaining no genuine benefit; knowing the true as true and seeing the false as false — this is right understanding, certain to gain genuine benefit.

The epistemic pair: right view sees clearly; wrong view inverts. The consequences follow automatically — not as punishment but as the natural fruit of accurate versus inaccurate perception.

盖屋不密，天雨则漏，意不惟行，淫泆为穿。盖屋善密，雨则不漏，摄意惟行，淫泆不生。

屋顶盖得不密实，下雨就会漏水，内心不思惟修行，贪欲就会穿透进来；屋顶盖得严密，下雨就不会漏，摄持内心思惟修行，贪欲就不会生起。

A poorly thatched roof leaks in rain; a mind not cultivated in practice is penetrated by lust. A well-thatched roof keeps out the rain; a mind guarded in practice does not produce lust.

One of the oldest and most enduring images in the Dhammapada: the leaky roof as unguarded mind. Practice is the thatch that, when applied thoroughly, keeps out the rain of desire.

鄙夫染人，如近臭物，渐迷习非，不觉成恶。贤夫染人，如近香熏，进智习善，行成洁芳。

卑鄙的人影响他人，就像靠近臭物，渐渐迷惑习于不正，不知不觉就成了恶；贤善的人影响他人，就像靠近香薰，进德修智习于善法，行为成就清净芬芳。

A base person influences others like proximity to something foul — gradually confused and habituated to wrong, becoming evil without realizing; a wise person influences others like proximity to incense — advancing in wisdom, habituated to goodness, conduct becoming pure and fragrant.

Companionship as contagion — in both directions. The metaphor of fragrance and foulness is precise: influence works through proximity and habituation, not through dramatic confrontation.

造忧后忧，行恶两忧，彼忧惟惧，见罪心懅。造喜后喜，行善两喜，彼喜惟欢，见福心安。

现在造忧后来也忧，行恶导致两世皆忧，那种忧惧充满了恐惧，见到自己的罪业而心生惶恐；现在造喜后来也喜，行善导致两世皆喜，那种喜乐充满了欢欣，见到自己的福德而心安。

Creating sorrow now and sorrow later — doing evil brings sorrow in both; that sorrow is filled with dread, seeing one's sin with a troubled mind. Creating joy now and joy later — doing good brings joy in both; that joy is filled with delight, seeing one's merit with a peaceful mind.

The psychological consequences of action extend beyond the action's immediate moment. The person who has acted harmfully carries a troubled mind; the one who has acted well carries peace. This is karma experienced as inner weather.

今悔后悔，为恶两悔，厥为自殃，受罪热恼。今欢后欢，为善两欢，厥为自祐，受福悦豫。

现在后悔，以后也后悔，行恶导致两世悔恨，这是自招的灾殃，承受罪业的热恼；现在欢喜，以后也欢喜，行善导致两世欢喜，这是自己的庇护，享受福德的悦豫。

Regretting now and regretting later — doing evil means regret in both; this is self-inflicted calamity, enduring the burning anguish of sin. Rejoicing now and rejoicing later — doing good means joy in both; this is self-given shelter, enjoying the delight of merit.

The experience of sin as 'burning anguish' (热恼) is visceral and precise: the memory of harmful action is not neutral — it generates ongoing heat. The experience of good action is shelter: a source of ongoing warmth rather than burning.

巧言多求，放荡无戒，怀淫怒痴，不惟止观，聚如群牛，非佛弟子。时言少求，行道如法，除淫怒痴，觉正意解，见对不起，是佛弟子。

言语巧妙却多求，放荡无戒，心怀淫欲、嗔怒、愚痴，不修止观，聚合如群牛，这样的人不是佛弟子；言语应时而少欲，行道如法，除去淫欲嗔怒愚痴，觉悟正意而解脱，见到对境不起（烦恼），这才是佛弟子。

Clever speech and many desires; undisciplined and unrestrained; harboring lust, anger, and delusion; not practising stillness and insight — like a herd of cattle, not disciples of the Buddha. Timely speech and few desires; practising the path as taught; removing lust, anger, and delusion; awakening right mind and liberation; when meeting objects, not arising — these are disciples of the Buddha.

The closing pair of the Pairs chapter defines, in stark contrast, what makes someone a genuine disciple. The herd of cattle image is striking — a mass without direction or individual awakening. The Buddha's disciple is defined by non-arising: when meeting the objects of the senses, nothing flares up.

总结 · Summary

双要品二十二颂，以对偶的形式呈现善恶因果的核心法则。从心念恶善到言行果报，从风靡草对风动山，从鄙夫熏人对贤夫熏人，每一对比都在揭示同一个真理：心念是一切的根本，善恶之行各有两界的果报，非佛弟子与佛弟子的区别，在于遇境是否生起（烦恼）。

The chapter of the Pairs presents twenty-two verses in a series of carefully constructed contrasts — evil/good mind, disordered/right conduct, unrooted grass/mountain in wind, base companionship/wise companionship, regret/joy. Each contrast illuminates the same fundamental truth: mind is the origin, and the quality of one's inner life produces its exact corresponding outer life. The chapter closes with a striking definition of the Buddhist disciple: one who, meeting objects, does not arise.
