多闻能持固，奉法为垣墙，精进难逾毁，从是戒慧成。多闻令志明，已明智慧增，智则博解义，见义行法安。多闻能除忧，能以定为欢，善说甘露法，自致得泥洹。闻为知法律，解疑亦见正，从闻舍非法，行到不死处。为能师现道，解疑令学明，亦兴清净本，能奉持法藏。能摄为解义，解则义不穿，受法猗法者，从是疾得安。若多少有闻，自大以憍人，是如盲执烛，照彼不自明。夫求爵位财，尊贵升天福，辩慧世间悍，斯闻为第一。帝王聘礼闻，天上天亦然，闻为第一藏，最富旅力强。智者为闻屈，好道者亦乐，王者尽心事，虽释梵亦然。仙人常敬闻，况贵巨富人，是以慧为贵，可礼无过是。事日为明故，事父为恩故，事君以力故，闻故事道人。人为命事医，欲胜依豪强，法在智慧处，福行世世明。察友在为谋，别伴在急时，观妻在房乐，欲知智在说。闻为今世利，妻子昆弟友，亦致后世福，积闻成圣智。是能散忧恚，亦除不祥衰，欲得安隐吉，当事多闻者。斫创无过忧，射箭无过愚，是壮莫能拔，唯从多闻除。盲从是得眼，闇者从得烛，亦导世间人，如目将无目。是故可舍痴，离慢豪富乐，务学事闻者，是名积聚德。

逐句解释

多闻能持固，奉法为垣墙，精进难逾毁，从是戒慧成。

广博的闻思能够坚固持守，以奉持正法为围墙保护自心，精进修行难以被摧毁，由此戒与慧皆能成就。

Extensive hearing steadfastly maintains one's grasp; upholding the Dharma as a rampart — diligence is hard to breach; from this both virtue and wisdom are perfected.

The opening verse compares the Dharma to a protective wall. Extensive learning (bahusuta) is not mere accumulation — it builds the inner architecture that holds against the storms of passion and circumstance.

多闻令志明，已明智慧增，智则博解义，见义行法安。

广学多闻使志向明确，志向明确则智慧增长，智慧能广泛理解法义，见到法义便安住于法行之中。

Extensive hearing clarifies resolve; clarity increases wisdom; wisdom broadly comprehends meaning; seeing meaning, one rests secure in practising the Dharma.

A chain of development: hearing → clarity of resolve → wisdom → understanding of meaning → stable practice. Each stage opens the next. The final resting point — secure in practice — is the practical fruit of all the hearing.

多闻能除忧，能以定为欢，善说甘露法，自致得泥洹。

广学多闻能除去忧虑，能以禅定为喜乐，善于宣说甘露之法，自然而然地到达涅槃。

Extensive hearing removes sorrow; it brings joy through meditative stillness; it enables one to teach the nectar-like Dharma; by this one naturally attains Nirvana.

Three fruits of deep learning: the removal of sorrow, the access to meditative joy, and the ability to share the teaching with others. The progression moves from personal benefit to communal benefit — receiving, then transmitting.

闻为知法律，解疑亦见正，从闻舍非法，行到不死处。

通过闻思来了解法律戒规，解除疑惑也能见到正道，从闻思中舍弃非法之行，修行到达不死之境。

Through hearing one knows the precepts and rules; doubt is resolved and the right way is seen; through hearing one abandons what is contrary to the Dharma; practice leads to the deathless.

Hearing the teaching serves multiple functions simultaneously: it clarifies the rules of conduct, it answers doubt, it corrects wrong views, and it points the direction of practice. The deathless is not given by the teaching — it is reached by the walking.

为能师现道，解疑令学明，亦兴清净本，能奉持法藏。

能够担任老师，为弟子显示修道之路，解除疑惑使修学者明白，又能建立清净之本，能够奉持法藏。

One who can reveal the path as a teacher, resolve doubt and enlighten students, and also establish the foundation of purity — such a one is able to uphold the treasury of the Dharma.

The mark of genuine learning: the capacity to transmit. The teacher who can show the path, answer questions, and embody purity is not just a scholar but a living embodiment of the Dharma treasury.

能摄为解义，解则义不穿，受法猗法者，从是疾得安。

能够摄持而理解法义，理解了则法义不会穿漏遗失，受法依法而住的人，从此能迅速得到安乐。

One who grasps and understands the meaning — when understood, the meaning does not leak away; those who receive and rely on the Dharma find peace swiftly.

Understanding that has been properly digested does not drain away. The person who has truly understood — not merely memorised — and who then lives in accordance with that understanding, finds peace without delay.

若多少有闻，自大以憍人，是如盲执烛，照彼不自明。

若只学了一点点，便自高自大骄慢于人，这就像瞎子拿着蜡烛，照亮别人却无法照见自己。

If one has heard only a little and yet becomes proud and arrogant toward others — this is like a blind person holding a lamp: it illuminates others but not oneself.

The image of the blind person with a lamp is one of the great images of Buddhist literature. It perfectly describes the condition of the person who can recite and explain teachings without having internalized them — lighting the way for others while remaining in their own darkness.

夫求爵位财，尊贵升天福，辩慧世间悍，斯闻为第一。

无论是追求官爵、财富，还是尊贵、升天之福，还是世间的辩才与智慧，多闻都是第一要素。

Whether seeking rank, wealth, honour, heavenly rebirth, eloquence, or worldly wisdom — extensive hearing stands first among all means.

The verse is pragmatic: whatever one's aspiration — worldly success, heavenly rebirth, or wisdom — extensive learning in the Dharma serves as the foundation. It is not presented as an end in itself but as the means that supports all legitimate ends.

帝王聘礼闻，天上天亦然，闻为第一藏，最富旅力强。

帝王以礼聘请多闻者，天上诸天也是如此，多闻是第一宝藏，是最富足、力量最强的资粮。

Kings seek out the learned with ceremonial respect; so do the gods — hearing is the foremost treasury, the richest wealth and the greatest strength for the journey.

The social verification is interesting: even kings and gods seek out those of genuine learning. But the verse shifts quickly from social prestige to something more fundamental — hearing is a treasury and a form of strength that sustains the entire spiritual journey.

智者为闻屈，好道者亦乐，王者尽心事，虽释梵亦然。

智者为了多闻而甘愿屈身低就，好道之人也因多闻而欢喜，王者尽心侍奉多闻者，乃至帝释、梵天也是如此。

The wise humble themselves for the sake of hearing; those who love the path rejoice in it; even kings devote themselves to the learned — and so do Indra and Brahma.

The inversion of social hierarchy is deliberate: those with the most worldly power willingly subordinate themselves to those with genuine knowledge. This is the proper social relationship — power serves wisdom, not the other way around.

仙人常敬闻，况贵巨富人，是以慧为贵，可礼无过是。

连仙人都常常尊敬多闻之人，何况贵族和大富翁？因此以智慧为最贵重，没有比这更值得礼敬的了。

Even sages habitually honour the learned — how much more so the noble and the wealthy. Therefore wisdom is the highest treasure; nothing is more worthy of reverence.

The argument moves by ascending status: ordinary people, then sages, then the most privileged. If even sages bow before genuine wisdom, the conclusion is clear — wisdom is the most valuable thing in the world, and honouring it is the highest form of respect.

事日为明故，事父为恩故，事君以力故，闻故事道人。

侍奉太阳是为了光明，侍奉父亲是因为恩情，侍奉国君是因为力量，因为多闻之故而侍奉道人。

We revere the sun for its light; we serve our father for his kindness; we serve our lord for power; we serve the person of the path because of the hearing they offer.

Each relationship is explained by what it gives. The spiritual teacher is placed last not because least important but because the reason for serving them is the highest — the gift of the teaching itself, which surpasses light, kindness, and power.

人为命事医，欲胜依豪强，法在智慧处，福行世世明。

人为了保命而侍奉医生，为了取胜而依靠豪强，但正法在智慧之所在处，修福之行世世明亮。

People seek a doctor to preserve life; they rely on the powerful to win — but the Dharma dwells where wisdom is; acts of merit illuminate every future life.

The verse makes a comparison between different types of reliance. The doctor and the powerful patron both address immediate, contingent needs. The Dharma, located in wisdom, addresses the fundamental level of existence — not one life but all lives.

察友在为谋，别伴在急时，观妻在房乐，欲知智在说。

观察朋友要在共谋事情时，辨别伴侣要在紧急关头，了解妻子要在家室之乐时，想知道一个人是否有智慧就看他的言说。

A friend is tested in counsel; a companion in times of crisis; a spouse in domestic life; wisdom is known through speech.

A practical list of how to see the truth about different relationships. The final item — wisdom revealed through speech — connects to the chapter's theme: genuine learning expresses itself in how one speaks, what one teaches, how one explains.

闻为今世利，妻子昆弟友，亦致后世福，积闻成圣智。

多闻对今世有利，使妻子兄弟朋友皆得益，也成就来世的福报，积累多闻能成就圣者的智慧。

Hearing benefits one's present life — one's spouse, siblings, and friends — and also creates merit for the next life; accumulated hearing builds the wisdom of the noble ones.

The benefits of learning ripple outward: first to oneself, then to one's household and social circle, then to future lives, and finally to the highest spiritual attainment. Good learning is inherently relational and generational.

是能散忧恚，亦除不祥衰，欲得安隐吉，当事多闻者。

多闻能够散去忧虑嗔恚，也能除去不祥和衰败，想要得到安稳和吉祥，应当侍奉多闻之人。

It disperses sorrow and anger; it also removes misfortune and decline; those who wish for safety and auspiciousness should associate with the extensively learned.

The practical consequences of associating with the learned: the negative states (sorrow, anger, misfortune) diminish, and stability increases. This is the social argument for learning communities — the benefit is shared, not merely individual.

斫创无过忧，射箭无过愚，是壮莫能拔，唯从多闻除。

刀创之痛没有比忧愁更深的，弓箭之伤没有比愚痴更大的，这些力量之盛难以拔除，唯有多闻才能去除它们。

No wound cuts deeper than sorrow; no arrow strikes harder than folly. These forces are so strong they cannot be pulled out — only extensive hearing can remove them.

The medical metaphor is extended: sorrow and stupidity are the deepest wounds, lodged too deep to be pulled out by force. They require the solvent of understanding — hearing the Dharma patiently until the root of delusion is dissolved.

盲从是得眼，闇者从得烛，亦导世间人，如目将无目。

瞎子因多闻而得眼，黑暗中的人因多闻而得到烛光，多闻也能引导世间的人，如同有眼者引领无眼者。

The blind gain sight from it; those in darkness receive a lamp; it also guides people in the world, as the sighted lead the blind.

The image of hearing as sight is among the oldest in contemplative literature. The teaching does not create the capacity to see — it restores what was always there by removing the darkness of ignorance. And those who have received this gift are immediately called to guide others.

是故可舍痴，离慢豪富乐，务学事闻者，是名积聚德。

因此应当舍弃愚痴，远离骄慢、财富与享乐，致力于修学、侍奉多闻之人，这才叫做积聚功德。

Therefore abandon stupidity; leave behind pride, wealth, and pleasure-seeking; devote yourself to learning and serving the learned — this is what is called accumulating virtue.

The chapter closes with a practical programme: shed the three obstacles (stupidity, pride, pleasure-seeking), seek out genuine teachers, and commit to sustained learning. This, not external accumulation, is what it means to build virtue.

总结 · Summary

多闻品十九颂，赞叹闻思修的核心地位。广博闻思是一切功德的基础：它坚固戒慧，解除疑惑，去除忧愁，开启智慧，引导他人，并最终通向涅槃。品末警告：少闻而骄慢者，如盲持灯，只照别人而不能自明。真正的多闻，需结合谦逊、精进与实践，方能成就圣者之智。

The chapter on Extensive Hearing praises hearing, study, and reflection as the indispensable foundation of all virtue and wisdom. Nineteen verses trace the fruits of genuine learning — from clarity of mind and removal of doubt to the capacity to teach others and ultimately reach Nirvana. The chapter also issues a warning: a little learning combined with pride is like a blind person holding a lamp, illuminating others while remaining in personal darkness. True learning requires humility, sustained effort, and practical application.
