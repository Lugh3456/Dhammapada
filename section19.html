第十九章 · 老耗品

法句经 · Dhammapada

← 第十八章 目录 Contents 第二十章 →

何有笑与乐，命常炽然，深弊幽冥，不求锭光？见身如沫，幻法自然，断魔华敷，不睹生死。此身为死物，精神无形法，假令死复生，罪福不败亡。日老毛鬓白，但坐食国恩，行无所积，何以当终？犹如古城，中无储积，有老无死，何以灭苦？老者形枯灭，何所考其真，形既若空虚，精神亦如幻。吾今何老矣，形败则神去，非老终其身，假以灭苦。生而当老，命数有限，受老行苦，此道不避。惟老之至，色白头秃，形气虚虐，而欲何求？胁行缩身，喘息屡顿，能以自强，修念正定。若人寿百岁，遄死不见法，不如生一日，一心学正定。若人寿百岁，邪伪无有智，不如生一日，一心学正智。

逐句解释

何有笑与乐，命常炽然，深弊幽冥，不求锭光？

有什么好笑和快乐的呢？生命常常炽燃，深陷弊暗幽冥，却不求光明之灯？

What is there to laugh about, what joy? When life is ever aflame; shrouded in deep darkness and obscurity — why not seek a lamp of light?

A stark opening: the premise of ordinary happiness is examined against the background of suffering. Life is aflame with aging and death; the lamp of wisdom is not sought.

见身如沫，幻法自然，断魔华敷，不睹生死。

观见身体如泡沫，幻法自然如此，截断魔的花朵（诱惑），不再被生死束缚。

See the body as foam; illusion-dharma is its nature; cut off the flowering of Mara — one no longer sees birth and death.

Three images: body as foam (fragile), all phenomena as illusion, and Mara's attractive displays as flowers to be severed.

此身为死物，精神无形法，假令死复生，罪福不败亡。

此身终将是死亡之物，精神属于无形之法，假设死后再生，罪与福都不会消灭。

This body is a thing of death; the spirit belongs to the formless Dharma; even if after death one is reborn — merit and fault do not perish.

Karma's persistence through death and rebirth: the body dies, but the moral weight of actions travels with the continuum.

日老毛鬓白，但坐食国恩，行无所积，何以当终？

日渐老去，头发发白，只是坐食国家恩惠，行为上没有积累（善行），以何度过最终（死亡）？

Day by day aging, the hair turning white; only sitting and consuming the nation's provisions; having accumulated nothing by conduct — with what will you face the end?

A pointed challenge to those who merely subsist without cultivating virtue. The end (death) will come; what has been accumulated to meet it?

犹如古城，中无储积，有老无死，何以灭苦？

就如同古老的城市，其中没有储积，有老却没有（超越）死，以何灭除苦？

Like an ancient city with nothing stored inside — there is aging but no transcendence of death. With what can suffering be ended?

The city-without-stores metaphor: a life lacking the accumulated merit and wisdom of practice has nothing to draw on when facing death.

老者形枯灭，何所考其真，形既若空虚，精神亦如幻。

老者形体枯竭消灭，何处去追寻其真实，形体既然如同空虚，精神也如同幻化。

When the aged one's form withers and perishes — where can its truth be sought? Since the form is already like emptiness, the spirit too is like illusion.

Both body and mind are found to be insubstantial on examination. This is not nihilism but the clear seeing of impermanence.

吾今何老矣，形败则神去，非老终其身，假以灭苦。

我如今这般老矣，形体衰败则神识离去，不是老而终其一身，而是借此灭除苦难。

How old am I now — when form collapses, the spirit departs; it is not that aging ends the body; rather, one uses this to extinguish suffering.

The practitioner reframes aging: not as mere decline but as an opportunity to recognize impermanence and practice toward liberation.

生而当老，命数有限，受老行苦，此道不避。

生而必然老去，生命的数量是有限的，接受老去而行于苦，此道不可回避。

Born to grow old; the measure of life is limited; receiving old age and walking in suffering — this path cannot be avoided.

The universal human condition stated plainly. The point is not to dramatize it but to face it honestly.

惟老之至，色白头秃，形气虚虐，而欲何求？

只是老迈至此，面色苍白、头发脱落，形气虚弱，而还想追求什么呢？

Only this: aged and arrived at this point — pallor, baldness, form and breath failing — and yet, what is there to desire?

A rhetorical question to the aged person: looking at the body in its decline, what exactly do you think you're going to achieve through craving?

胁行缩身，喘息屡顿，能以自强，修念正定。

侧行而身体蜷缩，喘息时常停顿，（若）能以此自强，修习念正定。

Walking sideways with body bent; breath often stopping — one who can use this to strengthen themselves, cultivating mindfulness and correct stillness.

Even in physical decline, meditation remains possible. The bent body and labored breath become the very subject of mindfulness practice.

若人寿百岁，遄死不见法，不如生一日，一心学正定。

若人活到百岁，却在死时还未见法，不如生活一日，一心学习正定。

If a person lives a hundred years, but at death has not seen the Dharma — better to live one day with wholehearted cultivation of correct stillness.

Quality over quantity of life: one day of real practice is more valuable than a century of heedless existence.

若人寿百岁，邪伪无有智，不如生一日，一心学正智。

若人活到百岁，却邪伪而没有智慧，不如生活一日，一心学习正智。

If a person lives a hundred years, but lives falsely, without wisdom — better to live one day with wholehearted cultivation of correct wisdom.

The parallel verse: not just stillness but wisdom (paññā). The recurring 'better one day' structure emphasizes the urgency of practice.

总结 · Summary

老耗品十二颂，以身体的衰败为镜，照见修行的紧迫。生命炽然，有笑可寻？身如泡沫，精神如幻，罪福不灭。头白形枯而不修，所积者何？百岁而不见法，不如一日一心正定。老耗品不哀叹老去，而是以老去为师。

Old Age presents twelve verses using the body's decline as a mirror for practice. Life is aflame, the body is foam, karma persists through death and rebirth. The aged person who has accumulated nothing faces death unprepared. But even in physical decline, one day of genuine practice surpasses a hundred years of heedless life. The chapter does not lament aging — it uses aging as a teacher.
